\begin{definition}[constructibleTopology] Let \(X\) be a topological space. The \emph{constructible topology} on \(X\) is the topology generated by the subbasis
\(\mathcal B\) consisting of compact open subsets of \(X\) and their complements.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[compactSpace_withConstructibleTopology]
If \(X\) is a quasi-separated, quasi-sober, prespectral and quasi-compact topological space, then \(X\)
is still quasi-compact in the constructible topology.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal B\) be the constructible subbasis. It suffices by the Alexander subbasis theorem to show that
every family
\[
P \subseteq \mathcal B
\]
with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection.

Assume for contradiction that this fails. Define
\[
\mathcal S
:=
\Bigl\{
P \subseteq \mathcal B :
\text{every finite } Q \subseteq P \text{ has } \bigcap Q \neq \varnothing,
\text{ but } \bigcap P = \varnothing
\Bigr\}.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal S\) is the collection of counterexamples to compactness of the constructible
topology. By assumption, \(\mathcal S\neq\varnothing\). By Zorn's lemma, we can choose a maximal element \(B \in \mathcal S\).

Indeed, if \(c\) is a nonempty chain in \(\mathcal S\), then \(\bigcup c\) is again in
\(\mathcal S\): it is still contained in \(\mathcal B\), its total intersection is empty,
and every finite subfamily of \(\bigcup c\) is already contained in one member of the chain,
hence has nonempty intersection.

Now let
\[
B' := \{\, s \in B : s \text{ is closed in } X \,\},
\qquad
Z := \bigcap B'.
\]
Since every member of \(B'\) is closed, \(Z\) is closed. Also \(Z\neq\varnothing\):
indeed, every finite subfamily of \(B'\) has nonempty intersection because \(B\) has the finite
intersection property, and since \(X\) is compact, the total intersection of the family \(B'\)
is nonempty.

We now distinguish two cases.

\noindent\textbf{Case 1: \(Z\) is irreducible.}

Since \(X\) is quasi-sober and \(Z\) is irreducible closed, \(Z\) has a generic point. Let \(\eta\) be that generic point. We claim that \(\eta \in \bigcap B\), which contradicts \(\bigcap B=\varnothing\). We show \(\eta \in i\) for every $i \in B$. Let $i \in B$. 

If \(i\) is closed, then \(i \in B'\), and since \(\eta \in Z=\bigcap B'\), we have \(\eta \in i\).

Suppose now that \(i\) is not closed. Since \(i \in B \subseteq \mathcal B\), it follows from the
definition of \(\mathcal B\) that \(i\) is open and compact.
Because \(\eta\) is a generic point of the irreducible closed set \(Z\), for every open set
\(U \subseteq X\) one has
\[
\eta \in U
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U \cap Z \neq \varnothing.
\]
Thus it is enough to show that \(i \cap Z \neq \varnothing\).

But \(i\) is compact, and \(Z=\bigcap B'\) is an intersection of closed sets. For every finite
family \(a \subseteq B'\), the intersection
\[
i \cap \bigcap a
\]
is nonempty, because \(a \cup \{i\}\) is a finite subfamily of \(B\), and \(B\) has the finite
intersection property. Therefore compactness of \(i\) implies that
\[
i \cap \bigcap B' \neq \varnothing,
\]
that is,
\[
i \cap Z \neq \varnothing.
\]
Hence \(\eta \in i\).

Since this holds for every \(i \in B\), we conclude that \(\eta \in \bigcap B\), which is a contradiction.

\noindent\textbf{Case 2: \(Z\) is reducible.}

Since \(Z\) is a nonempty closed subset of the prespectral space \(X\) and is not irreducible,
there exist closed subsets \(Y_1, Y_2 \subseteq X\) such that:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \(Z \subseteq Y_1 \cup Y_2\),
  \item \(X \setminus Y_1\) and \(X \setminus Y_2\) are compact,
  \item \(Z \nsubseteq Y_1\) and \(Z \nsubseteq Y_2\).
\end{itemize}

In particular,
\[
Y_1, Y_2 \in \mathcal B,
\]
because they are closed with compact complements.

Also,
\[
Y_1 \cap \bigcap B = \varnothing,
\qquad
Y_2 \cap \bigcap B = \varnothing,
\]
since \(\bigcap B=\varnothing\).

By maximality of \(B\), neither \(B \cup \{Y_1\}\) nor \(B \cup \{Y_2\}\) can belong to
\(\mathcal S\). Since both are still contained in \(\mathcal B\), and since their total
intersections are empty, the only way they can fail to lie in \(\mathcal S\) is that the
finite intersection property fails. Hence there exist finite families
\[
A_1 \subseteq B \cup \{Y_1\},
\qquad
A_2 \subseteq B \cup \{Y_2\},
\]
such that
\[
Y_1 \cap \bigcap (A_1 \setminus \{Y_1\}) = \varnothing,
\qquad
Y_2 \cap \bigcap (A_2 \setminus \{Y_2\}) = \varnothing.
\]

Set
\[
W := \bigcap\bigl((A_1 \setminus \{Y_1\}) \cup (A_2 \setminus \{Y_2\})\bigr).
\]
We first claim that
\[
Z \cap W = \varnothing.
\]

Indeed, let \(x \in Z \cap W\). Since \(x \in Z \subseteq Y_1 \cup Y_2\), we have either
\(x \in Y_1\) or \(x \in Y_2\). If \(x \in Y_1\), then \(x \in W\) implies \(x \in \bigcap(A_1 \setminus \{Y_1\})\), hence
\[
x \in Y_1 \cap \bigcap(A_1 \setminus \{Y_1\}),
\]
contradicting the choice of \(A_1\). Similarly, if \(x \in Y_2\), then
\[
x \in Y_2 \cap \bigcap(A_2 \setminus \{Y_2\}),
\]
contradicting the choice of \(A_2\). Thus \(Z \cap W = \varnothing\).

On the other hand, \(W\) is compact. Indeed, note that every member of \(\mathcal B\) is compact. If \(s \in \mathcal B\) is open compact, then it is immediate and if it is closed, then it is a closed subset of compact space \(X\) which is also compact. Since \(X\) is quasi-separated and \(W\) is a finite intersection of members of
\(\mathcal B\) which are all compact, \(W\) is compact as well.

Now let \(F \subseteq B'\) be a finite subfamily. Then
\[
F \cup (A_1 \setminus \{Y_1\}) \cup (A_2 \setminus \{Y_2\})
\]
is a finite subfamily of \(B\), hence has nonempty intersection by the defining property of
\(B\). This means exactly that
\[
W \cap \bigcap F \neq \varnothing.
\]

Since \(W\) is compact and every member of \(B'\) is closed, it follows that
\[
W \cap \bigcap B' \neq \varnothing.
\]
But \(\bigcap B' = Z\), so
\[
W \cap Z \neq \varnothing,
\]
contradicting the previously established equality \(Z \cap W = \varnothing\).

Both cases lead to contradictions. Therefore \(\mathcal S = \varnothing\). Hence every family
of subbasic constructible opens with the finite intersection property has nonempty total
intersection. By the Alexander subbasis theorem, \(X\) is compact in the constructible topology.
\end{proof}
