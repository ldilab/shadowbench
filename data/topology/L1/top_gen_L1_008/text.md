\begin{definition}[paths_homotopic]
A topological space $X$ is simply connected if its fundamental groupoid is equivalent to the the groupoid with one object and the identity morphism.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[simply_connected_iff_paths_homotopic]
A topological space is simply connected if and only if it is path connected, and there is at most one path up to homotopy between any two points.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $X$ be a simply connected space. The fundamental groupoid $\Pi X$ being equivalent to a groupoid $\mathcal{E}$ with one object $e$ and the identity morphism, is equivalent to being a groupoid having a unique morphism between any two objects. It is equivalent to say that any two points of $X$ have a unique path between them up to homotopy equivalence. In partiular, $X$ is path connected in such case.
\end{proof}
